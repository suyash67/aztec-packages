Barretenberg · Zcash Orchard Action circuit

Proving the Orchard Action in barretenberg

Four barretenberg implementations of Zcash's Orchard Action statement, two arithmetizations on two curve cycles, measured against the production halo2 prover that Zcash runs today.

Conclusions

  • Honk proves Zcash's own circuit about 3× faster. With the same curves (Pallas/Vesta), the same halo2 gates and the same IPA commitment scheme as Zcash, swapping halo2's quotient argument for Honk's Sumcheck cuts one Action from {{zcash:1:prove}} to {{halo2-honk-pasta:1:prove}} ({{ratio:zcash:halo2-honk-pasta:1:prove}}), and 16 Actions from {{zcash:16:prove}} to {{halo2-honk-pasta:16:prove}} ({{ratio:zcash:halo2-honk-pasta:16:prove}}). Single-threaded the gap is similar: {{zcash:1:prove_st}} against {{halo2-honk-pasta:1:prove_st}}.
  • Barretenberg proofs grow slowly with more Actions. Zcash's proof is the smallest for one Action ({{zcash:1:size}} against {{ultra-zk-pasta:1:size}} to {{ultra-zk-bn254:1:size}}), but it grows linearly with the number of Actions. Barretenberg puts all Actions in one circuit, so its proofs grow logarithmically and are smaller from 4 Actions on. At 16 Actions they are {{ultra-zk-pasta:16:size}} to {{halo2-honk-pasta:16:size}}, against {{zcash:16:size}}.
  • Custom wide gates beat a generic arithmetization. Orchard's 10-column halo2 layout fits an Action in {{halo2-honk-pasta:1:rows_n}} rows. Ultra's 4-wire gates need {{ratio:ultra-zk-pasta:halo2-honk-pasta:1:rows}} as many rows when written by hand ({{ultra-zk-pasta:1:rows_n}} gates), and {{ratio:ultra-zk-bn254:halo2-honk-bn254:1:rows}} as many through barretenberg's stdlib. On the same curve and commitment scheme, the Ultra prover is {{ratio:ultra-zk-pasta:halo2-honk-pasta:1:prove}} slower on Pasta and {{ratio:ultra-zk-bn254:halo2-honk-bn254:16:prove}} slower on BN254 at 16 Actions.
  • BN254 is faster but needs a trusted setup. Running the same halo2 gates on BN254 with KZG commitments proves another {{ratio:halo2-honk-pasta:halo2-honk-bn254:1:prove}} faster ({{halo2-honk-bn254:1:prove}} for one Action) and verifies in a constant ~7 ms. The cost is a trusted setup and about 100–110 bits of security, against about 126 bits for the transparent Pasta setup.
  • IPA verification grows with circuit size. As in halo2, the IPA verifier ends with one multi-scalar multiplication over all generators. It goes from {{halo2-honk-pasta:1:verify}} to {{halo2-honk-pasta:16:verify}} for the halo2-gate circuit (Zcash: {{zcash:1:verify}} to {{zcash:16:verify}}), and reaches {{ultra-zk-pasta:16:verify}} for the much larger Ultra circuit.
  • Best fit for Zcash: halo2 gates on Honk over Pasta. It keeps Zcash's curves, its transparent setup and its exact Orchard gates, and proves about 3× faster with smaller multi-Action proofs. Two optimizations remain open for all Pasta configurations: the GLV endomorphism and batched affine additions in multi-scalar multiplication.

Configurations

Every configuration proves the full Action statement (Merkle path, value commitment, nullifier, spend authority, diversified address, old and new note commitments, and the enable flags) with zero knowledge, and verifies the proof. The BN254 variants replace Pallas with Grumpkin as the embedded curve: Sinsemilla, Poseidon and the fixed bases are re-derived over the BN254 scalar field with the same domain strings, so they measure the cost of the same statement on that cycle rather than Zcash's exact instance.

Method and caveats

  • Machine: 4 vCPU Intel Xeon at 2.1 GHz. barretenberg is built with the default CMake preset (release, clang); Zcash with cargo --release. Medians of 3 to 5 runs after a warm-up.
  • Zcash's prover builds one circuit per Action and proves all of them in one create_proof; its proof grows linearly with the number of Actions. barretenberg proves one circuit holding all Actions.
  • BN254 configurations rely on a trusted setup and give roughly 100 to 110 bits of security; the Pasta configurations are transparent with roughly 126 bits, as Zcash.
  • The UltraHonk BN254 prover pads Sumcheck and Gemini to log n = 25 so that recursive verifiers have a fixed shape; this adds rounds to the proof and to verification but little prover work.
  • Pasta multi-scalar multiplications use a full-width bucket method without the GLV endomorphism, matching the technique of pasta_curves' multiexp. Both curves admit GLV; using it, and batched affine bucket additions, are open optimizations for every Pasta configuration.
  • The halo2 IPA prover computes the first four rounds as MSMs over the original generators and folds the generators explicitly afterwards; its verifier, like halo2's, does one MSM over all generators.
