Eight PCS backends for UltraHonk

Every backend proves and verifies the same UltraFlavor circuits (arithmetic + lookups + ROM, padded to dyadic sizes 2¹⁴–2²⁰) through one shared shell that swaps only the polynomial commitment scheme. Measured on an Apple M4 Pro (arm64 build, BN254 field assembly disabled), hash backends at λ=100 (conjectured), single-run timings. Reading the picture

Pairing backends own succinctness. Mercury's constant-size opening gives the smallest proofs at every size and flat ~4 ms verification at the same trust model as KZG; KZG+Shplemini remains the balanced incumbent. Hash backends own transparency at scale. Ligero is the fastest prover through 2¹⁸; WHIR trades prover time for flat verification and MiB-not-multi-MiB proofs, and is the recursion-friendly transparent option. The transparent-EC corner splits three ways. IPA has the smallest transparent proofs but a linear verifier (and a deliberately naive prover here); Hyrax is the simplest √N scheme with deterministic verification; Dory compresses commitments to a single GT element with only two pairings to verify. KZH2 is the accumulation play — its numbers here are one-shot, but its structure (linear checks, no FFTs in opening) is what KZH-Fold exploits for sublinear IVC.

Caveats

Timings are single-iteration (±10–20%) on a machine whose disabled field assembly slows MSMs and Poseidon2 relative to Blake3 hashing; ratios shift ~2× on deployment hardware. KZH2 and Dory run on documented test-only setups (deterministic trapdoors / scalar-derived G2 generators) with production-identical runtime cost. The IPA prover uses naive generator folding (its verifier and proof numbers are representative; its prover is not). WHIR/Ligero query counts use the up-to-capacity conjecture, no grinding. Sources and per-backend protocol documentation live in barretenberg/cpp/src/barretenberg/commitment_schemes/ (README/RECURSION/PCS_CANDIDATES) on branch sb/whir.
